City Council Approves New Metro Line Linking Airport to Old Town

The Riverton City Council voted 9 to 3 on Tuesday to approve a new 18-kilometre metro line that will connect Riverton International Airport with the Old Town district. Mayor Priya Nair said the project would cost 4,500 crore rupees and is scheduled to open in 2029.

Construction is expected to begin in June 2026, with the first phase covering 7 kilometres between the airport and Central Junction. The line will have 14 stations, and officials estimate it will carry 250,000 passengers every day once it is fully operational.

Transport commissioner Dr. Arvind Kulkarni told reporters that travel time between the airport and Old Town will fall from 70 minutes by road to 25 minutes by metro. He added that the project is expected to create 8,000 jobs during construction and about 1,200 permanent jobs afterwards.

Not everyone welcomed the decision. Council member Rahul Deshmukh, who voted against the plan, argued that the budget was too high and that money should first be spent on repairing existing roads. Farmers near the airport corridor also raised concerns about land acquisition. The state government said every affected family would receive compensation at twice the market rate and a guaranteed housing plot.

Environmental groups gave the plan a cautious welcome. The Riverton Green Alliance said the metro could remove nearly 40,000 cars from the roads each day, but it asked the council to protect the 300 mature trees along the planned route. The council promised that two new trees would be planted for every tree removed.

The project will be funded through a combination of state grants, a loan from a development bank and central government support. A final tender for the construction contract is expected to be published next month. Residents can submit comments on the route until 30 April through the city's public portal.
